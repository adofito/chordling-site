Guide and Support

Chordling keeps one song in three layers on one timeline: the chords, the melody, and the harmony parts on top. This guide walks through each, then covers settings, exporting and getting help.

For Chordling 1.0 on Mac, iPad and iPhone. Menu commands are the Mac’s; on iPad and iPhone the same actions are in the top bar and the Parts panel.

Songs

The song list is where every song lives. Tap + (New Song) for an empty song, or Start from Demo to open one of 20 demo songs, from a first scale to advanced harmony. Right-click or long-press a song to Rename or Delete it; deleting can’t be undone. There’s no limit on how many songs you keep.

The melody

The melody is written on the piano roll. With the Draw tool, drag across the grid to place a note; the tinted rows are the notes in your key. The Tools menu in the top bar switches between Draw, Select and Erase, and sets Snap to. Undo and redo are in the top bar too.

The chords

The chord row runs under the ruler. In an empty song it offers Add chord and a Start from menu of ten progressions to begin with, such as Pop I–V–vi–IV, Jazz ii–V–I, the 12-bar blues and the Andalusian cadence.

For the whole progression at once, right-click or long-press the Chords row in the Parts panel and choose Edit Progression…. The progression editor shows the chords four ways: Wheel, Map, Ladder and Voices. Its detail panel ends with Under the melody: the chords that contain the note your melody holds where the pick would land, and that note’s role in each.

Melody guides

To see which notes fit while you write, open the melody part’s settings and choose a level under Melody guides: Scale, Chord Tones, Guide Tones or Targets. The Chords part’s settings add Guides on the canvas, with chord guides and their strength.

The song’s form

The Form row above the ruler holds the song’s sections. Tap + to add one, then right-click or long-press it to set its kind (Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Solo, Outro), rename it, repeat it up to four times, duplicate it, split it at the playhead or move it.

Harmonize a melody

Open the Chords part’s settings and find Harmonize. Pick a Style and How often the chords change (Every bar, Every half bar or Follow the melody), then tap Harmonize. You get results to compare: play the song with each from the Hear strip (Song, A, B, C). Tap a chord in a result to read why it is there and Keep it; Harmonize again rewrites the rest. Apply puts a result into your song.

Simple and Diatonic sevenths are free. Borrowed colors, Secondary dominants, Tritone subs and jazz, Pedal point, Modal and Minor-key color come with Chordling Pro.

Analyze

Analyze, in the Chords part’s settings, says what each change does and the cadence it makes, for the chord playing or the one you select, drawn as a map or a wheel. Add chords first; it reads whatever the song holds.

Harmony parts

Add a part that harmonizes your melody with Add Harmony at the foot of the Parts panel (on Mac also Harmony › Add Harmony, ⇧⌘H). Its menu lists the blends: Classic blend (below), Wide blend (below), Blend above, Octave double, Follow the chords and Bass under melody. Empty Part gives you a part to write by hand. A part’s settings hold its name, color, sound and blend.

One harmony part is free. A second and third part, and the sung Voice sounds, come with Chordling Pro. A song holds up to three harmony parts.

Listen and Pitch Matching

Listen, the microphone button in the transport, shows the pitch you sing on the piano roll while it’s on. It’s free. The Pitch Matching Test (Settings › Pitch Matching › Start the Test…) grades how closely you match a sung or piano guide; it comes with Chordling Pro. Neither records anything: see the privacy policy.

The band

The Band section of the Chords part’s settings puts a bass line, drums and the keys’ rhythm under the song, from style presets (Rock trio, Bossa, Ballad, Jazz combo) or set row by row. Its lanes appear in the Parts panel with power, mute and solo. The band comes with Chordling Pro.

Settings

On Mac, Settings is its own window; on iPad it’s the gear in the top bar; on iPhone it’s in the More menu. It holds iCloud, Appearance, Language, Notation (scale degrees or letter names, and sharps or flats), Pitch Range, Pitch Matching, Timeline Ruler, Metronome, Playback, Bed and Tone, and ends with About Chordling.

Export and Fretling

  • Export Audio writes the song as an audio file (CAF). On Mac it’s File › Export Audio… (⇧⌘E); on iPad and iPhone the Export button opens the share sheet.
  • Export Progression (File › Export Progression…, ⌥⌘E) saves the song’s chords as a file that Fretling, the sister app for guitar, opens on its fretboard. Import Progression… (⌥⌘I) brings a progression back.

Chordling Pro

Locked features wear a small lock and stay in sight. Tap one to see what Pro holds, its price, Try Pro free for 14 days and Restore Purchases. One purchase covers Mac, iPad and iPhone and can be shared with your family. Details are on Free & Pro.

Help

The microphone doesn’t work

If you turned the microphone down, Chordling shows Microphone Access Required with a button to the right settings. On iPad and iPhone, turn on Microphone in Settings › Chordling. On Mac, enable Chordling in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. Then try Listen again.

My songs don’t appear on another device

Open Settings › iCloud on both devices. Account should read Signed in to iCloud; if it reads Not signed in to iCloud, sign in to iCloud in the system settings. Use the same Apple Account on each device, and give a new song a moment to sync.

I bought Pro on another device

Open any locked feature and tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with. A family member’s purchase reaches you through Family Sharing.

Something else

Write to adofito@gmail.com with your device, the app’s version (in About Chordling) and what happened.
